The case for 36mm

Sample post — replace with your own. The number on the spec sheet everyone reads first is the one that matters least.

Why this size

Watch sizing drifted upward for twenty years and is only now coming back. A 36mm case reads as "small" in the catalogue and as "correct" on most wrists under 7 inches — which is most wrists. The proportions that made the classic references classic were designed at this size.

The number that actually matters

Lug-to-lug. A 36mm watch with a 44mm lug-to-lug sits inside the wrist and disappears under a cuff; a 40mm watch with a 49mm lug-to-lug overhangs on the same wrist and never looks settled. Diameter tells you how big the dial looks; lug-to-lug tells you how the watch wears.

Where it falls down

Legibility on busy dials, and a short strap taper can look thin. And if your wrist is over 7.5 inches, none of this applies — go 38–39mm and ignore me.

Verdict

★★★★☆

If you're between two sizes, buy the smaller one. Try it on a leather strap before you decide it looks small.
